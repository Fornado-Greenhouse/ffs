Written: `docs/research/2026-09-14-entity-resolution.md` (210 lines, dense; nothing else touched, nothing committed).

Five most important findings:

1. **Wikidata separates identity from name from title.** The QID is opaque and permanent, labels and aliases are non-unique data, and the Wikipedia title is a changeable human string with parenthetical disambiguation. FFS's name-derived slug ids are Wikipedia titles, not QIDs; task_38 should mint opaque ids and keep human file names as a projection concern, with redirect stubs on rename.
2. **Merges redirect, never delete, and undo restores both sides.** Wikidata: "Merged items should be redirected. Never reuse merged items for other things"; RDF exposes `owl:sameAs`. ORCID is the cautionary case (deprecated record's data is deleted and cannot be reinstated). FFS should add `entity.same_as` and `entity.different_from` predicates, with undo by supersession, so a wrong merge costs nothing.
3. **Fellegi and Sunter (1969) two thresholds, three outcomes** is the rule every mature system uses; the middle band is clerical review. task_36's `resolution` needs a third value, `ambiguous`, with a candidate list, and ADR-029 must treat it as always-review. The W3C Reconciliation API's `match` flag is the same rule as an API shape.
4. **Coherence beats context beats prior.** AIDA (Hoffart 2011, ~82% on CoNLL-YAGO) and Bhattacharya and Getoor's collective ER both show that resolving all mentions in a document jointly is the strongest signal; the resolver should receive task_36's whole multi-entity envelope, with organization agreement weighted high and shared surname low.
5. **Notability is a NIL policy, and every review decision is a labeled pair.** Wikipedia covers the event, not the one-time person; FFS should mint a person only on a distinguishing attribute or a second sighting, append accepted mention text to aliases so the alias table grows from use, and record `different_from` only on an explicit owner click.

Unverified items are marked in the memo: exact accuracy numbers for Bunescu and Pasca 2006, Cucerzan 2007, and TAC-KBP 2011 (PDFs were not text-extractable), whether deleted QIDs or MBIDs can ever be reused, and Dedupe's typical labeled-pair count (docs returned 403).</result>

Five most important findings:

1. **Nobody coordinates who reads first.** All seven protocols make intake idempotent by a content key and dedup after the fact: ActivityPub drops repeated activity ids (§5.2), Nostr clients drop repeated event hashes, gossipsub keeps a seen cache. FFS should do the same: no lease, no leader; two peers reading the same email costs one fetch and yields one `same_as`.
2. **Re-share is a reference, never a copy.** ActivityPub `Announce`, gossipsub `IHAVE`/`IWANT`, IPFS CIDs. Five most important findings:

1. **Wikipedia never lets a review state claim more than it checked.** Pending-changes "accepted" means "checked for obvious problems"; verifiability, not truth, is the inclusion threshold; `{{As of}}` dates claims and collects them by month for re-check. 5. **Automated correction is precision-first and reversible.** ClueBot NG runs at 0.1 percent false positives for about 40 percent recall with a one-revert-per-day cap; subtle staleness survives months (median vandalism repair is minutes, the tail is years). FFS should do nothing automatic to an existing fact and instead schedule re-confirmation.
