# ADR-033: Federation deferred behind the intake pipeline

## Status

Proposed (owner decision pending)

## Date

2026-09-14

## Context

Bilateral federation is the MVP's differentiator. ADR-007 put personal federation in MVP, ADR-012 kept it pairwise, ADR-020 fixed the transport (mTLS over HTTPS, pull-based, fingerprint-pinned, capability-filtered at the source), and tasks 14 and 15 shipped it with integration tests. It is the thesis of the project: sub-record sharing between substrates you own.

The goal stated on 2026-09-14 is different in kind. Read the local business press every morning, keep the filing cabinets current on the movers and shakers, and derive what to do next. Tasks 38 to 45 implement it: registry-declared path families and the business-graph predicates (38), the scribe's extraction engines (36), the resolver (45), the courier (40), auto-filing (39), the briefing (41), and three spikes that gate them (42, 43, 44). Every one of those tasks is single-user. None of them exercises a bridge, a pull, a capability-filtered serve, or a revocation. The first federation use case that has a reason to exist, sharing a movers-and-shakers cabinet with a peer, does not exist until the cabinet does.

Build capacity is one person plus agents. Two tracks in parallel means neither reaches daily use. The plan needs to say which one waits, and for how long, so that the deferral is a decision and not a drift.

## Decision

Proposed, and it needs the owner's explicit yes: federation stays shipped and tested but receives no new work until the intake pipeline reaches daily use.

"Daily use" means task_41's briefing has been in use for two consecutive weeks: the courier runs unattended, the inbox (ADR-032) or the panel is worked most mornings, and the briefing is read.

While the deferral holds:

- Federation-relevant changes elsewhere in the codebase must keep the federation tests green. `cargo nextest run --workspace --all-features` stays the gate; no federation test is skipped or deleted.
- Changes must respect peer version skew. A peer on an older build must tolerate atoms with predicates it does not know. ADR-031 verified this for `affiliation` atoms: the store and the federation pull do not consult the registry at insert, so unknown predicates are stored and simply have no projection. New predicates from task_38 (`org.company`, `source.article`, `event.business`, `entity.same_as`, `entity.different_from`, `auditor.briefing`) inherit that behavior and must not break it.
- No new federation surface (RPC method, bridge handshake field, capability scope dimension) is added without reopening this ADR.

When the deferral lifts, three questions reopen with the intake pipeline's results in hand:

1. `entity.same_as` across peers. Your Sara Chen and a peer's Sara Chen have different opaque ids by design (ADR-030). Cross-substrate identity is a same-as assertion with two signers, and the resolver must decide whose candidates to trust.
2. Predicate vocabulary agreement. Two substrates that both annotate `affiliation` as a BFO role with a bearer and a context (ADR-031's `[ontology]` table) can map each other's predicates even when field names differ. ADR-007's bilateral federation has no vocabulary negotiation today.
3. The first real use case: sharing a cabinet. "You can see the orgs and affiliations I file from the business press; I can see yours; either of us can revoke" is the contact-sovereignty scenario from ADR-004 with content that actually accumulates.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep federation on the critical path

- **Description:** Interleave federation work (multi-peer aggregation from ADR-012's Phase 2 list, PSI, ZTM transport) with the intake tasks.
- **Pros:** The differentiator keeps moving; peer-version-skew problems surface early.
- **Cons:** There is no user for it. Nobody is federating an empty cabinet, and the intake pipeline is what fills the cabinet. Splitting capacity means the pipeline reaches daily use later, which delays the day federation has content to share.
- **Why rejected:** Federation without a cabinet is a demo. The cabinet without federation is a tool the owner uses every morning.

### Alternative 2: Remove federation from the MVP

- **Description:** Cut tasks 14 and 15 from the shipped surface, delete the crate, and revisit in Phase 3.
- **Pros:** Less to keep green; one fewer stable surface.
- **Cons:** It is built, tested, and it is the thesis (ADR-001, ADR-004, ADR-007). Deleting shipped, working code to save test time is not a saving.
- **Why rejected:** Deferral costs nothing; removal costs the differentiator.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One track, one definition of done, and a measurable trigger for resuming the other.
- The intake pipeline's outputs (opaque ids, `[ontology]` annotations, the resolver) are exactly the inputs the reopened federation questions need. Waiting improves the federation design instead of freezing it.

### Negative

- ADR-004's "all three scenarios ship in MVP" is not advanced for the duration. The clone-and-collaborate scenario in particular stays where tasks 14 and 15 left it.
- Peer-version-skew bugs introduced by the new predicates may sit undetected until federation resumes, because nobody is running two substrates. The unknown-predicate tolerance test from ADR-031 is the only guard.

### Risks

- **The deferral never lifts.** "Two weeks of daily use" slips as the pipeline grows features. **Mitigation:** the trigger is written here, in the governance index, and in the briefing task's success criteria; the auditor can report "briefing read N of the last 14 days" so the trigger is measured, not felt.
- **A federation-relevant regression lands unnoticed.** **Mitigation:** the workspace test gate and the skew rule above; any change to `crates/ffs-federation/` reopens this ADR.

## Implementation Notes

- No code changes. This ADR is a scheduling decision recorded where scheduling decisions live.
- The governance index adds this ADR to the `crates/ffs-federation/` row so anyone opening that crate reads the deferral first.
- task_41's success criteria should include the "two weeks of daily use" measurement so the trigger has an owner.
