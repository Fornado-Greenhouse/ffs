# ADR-035: The morning read is a session with the owner, not a scrape

## Status

Accepted (owner's decision, 2026-09-14)

The owner's words, verbatim: "let's be very careful, I don't want this to act as a bot performing a scrape. I want it to be my personal assistant, conducting our morning read."

## Date

2026-09-14

## Context

The newspaper goal (tasks 38 to 47) started from a picture of an agent that receives the Charlotte Business Journal digest, opens every link with the owner's logged-in session, downloads each article, and files what it finds. Spike task_43 tested that picture against reality and found four things, recorded in `docs/research/spikes/task-43-intake-reality.md`:

1. The CBJ digest email carries headlines and links only. No blurbs, no bylines, no names. Zero of fourteen items yield a person or an organization from the email alone. The one engineering gift is that the tracking links embed the real article URL as base64, so canonical URLs decode from the email with no click.
2. bizjournals.com and its RSS host answer 403 to any fetch that is not a browser. The owner's signed-in Chrome session renders full articles (ten paragraphs, about 2,900 characters for the sample), so authenticated browser access works technically.
3. ACBJ's User Agreement (revised 2024-08-13) prohibits using "any bots, cheats, macros, scripts ... or any other automated process" to access the service, and prohibits copying, extracting, or storing content for any purpose "related to data mining and/or the training or operation of any software or service to the extent that it incorporates a large language model." The stated remedy is termination of the account. The Charlotte Observer (McClatchy) and Axios Charlotte carry equivalent clauses.
4. Sources that permit personal automation or are public record exist and are rich: the CLTtoday newsletter (6AM City, personal-use license, full blurbs with names and dates; spike task_42 measured Claude Sonnet 5 at 1.00 precision on people and orgs over five of its emails), Mecklenburg County's permit FeatureServer (REST JSON, no key), Charlotte's Legistar Web API (JSON, no token), SEC EDGAR with a declared User-Agent, and newsroom RSS from Duke Energy and Truist. Two of those feeds already corroborated CBJ headlines from the same week.

ADR-026 drew the line that resolves this. The privacy question there was not "does data leave the machine" but "ambient versus invoked": a daemon that silently ships every note to an API is ambient, a user-configured engine choice is invoked. The same line applies to reading. A scheduled process that opens a subscriber's articles and stores their text is a scrape, whatever session it uses. A person reading the paper with an assistant beside them, opening one article at a time and deciding what to write down, is the owner's own use of the owner's own subscription. The publisher's terms name the first and not the second, and the owner has said plainly which one they want.

## Decision

### (1) Two separate things: the courier and the morning read

**The courier** is deterministic, scheduled, and touches only what may be automated:

- The owner's own mailbox. The CBJ digest is decoded into `source.article` pointers: title, url, date, publication, and nothing else. No content. CLTtoday items are filed with their blurb text and the parenthetical attribution each item carries, so ADR-034's quorum can discount a CLTtoday item that is itself a copy of a CBJ report.
- Public-record and permitted feeds: the Mecklenburg County permits FeatureServer, the Charlotte Legistar Web API, SEC EDGAR with a declared User-Agent and the published rate ceiling, newsroom RSS from publishers who intend their releases to be redistributed.

The courier never fetches from bizjournals.com, the Observer, or Axios. It has no browser, no cookie jar, no LLM client. task_40 builds it.

**The morning read** is an interactive session. The owner is present, invokes it, and reads at human pace. The assistant walks the day's pointers in order, opens each article in the owner's own signed-in browser one at a time and only when the owner says so, discusses it, and files what the owner wants remembered as the owner's own note: the people, organizations, affiliations, and events, and the owner's take. Never the article text. task_48 writes the skill.

### (2) Rules the assistant MUST follow during the read

- One article at a time, on the owner's cue. No prefetching, no background fetch, no scheduled fetch, no bulk operations. Human pace.
- No caching or storing of article bodies, in the substrate, in scratch files, or in the session log.
- Summaries are spoken in the session. Only the notes the owner chooses are filed.
- Provenance on every filed atom records the article url as the source and the session as the origin: `kind: morning_read`, the assistant's identity as the actor, and a flag that the owner was present.
- Because the owner is present and decides, filing from the read is the owner's accept. This is AARM Context-Dependent Allow with the human in the loop live, so proposals from the read may be accepted in-session without a separate quarantine pass. Each such accept auto-emits an ADR-034 attestation with basis `owner_knowledge` (the owner stated it) or `re_read_same_source` (the owner read it in the article).
- Anything the assistant infers rather than the owner states is marked as inference in the filed content, per ADR-027.
- The assistant refuses bulk requests ("open all of today's links", "summarize everything") and says why. Each article open is logged as a session event.

### (3) The read doubles as the review

The day's pending quarantine items (courier-filed pointers, CLTtoday extractions, permit and Council items, ambiguous resolutions from task_45's resolver, role changes routed by ADR-029) are walked in the same session. ADR-032's inbox file becomes the read's agenda. Spike task_44 therefore measures the read, not a solo review pass: the question is whether the owner would do this daily with an assistant, not whether they would tick forty boxes alone.

### (4) What is explicitly not built

- An unattended CBJ fetcher of any shape.
- A cookie-jar or headless-browser fetch path.
- LLM extraction over stored bizjournals.com, Observer, or Axios bodies.
- Any `fetch.mode` or per-source fetch flag for terms-restricted publishers. The option is removed from task_40, not left off by default.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Automated full fetch with the owner accepting the account risk

- **Description:** Build the authenticated fetch (cookie jar or the browser extension on a schedule), extract with the LLM engine, and treat the User Agreement as the owner's risk to carry.
- **Pros:** The original picture works end to end with no one present; the cabinet fills itself.
- **Cons:** It is the thing the publisher's terms name. The practical exposure is termination of a paid subscription the owner values. It also makes FFS's first real pipeline an example of the behavior the agent memory convention exists to prevent: an agent acting outside what the human sanctioned.
- **Why rejected:** Rejected by the owner, in the words quoted above.

### Alternative 2: Pointer-only with no reading assistance

- **Description:** The courier files CBJ titles and URLs and the briefing lists them; the owner reads alone and writes notes by hand if they want to.
- **Pros:** Nothing touches the publisher; simplest possible courier.
- **Cons:** Loses the goal. "Read the newspaper end to end and keep the filing cabinets current" was the point; a list of links the owner may or may not open is a bookmark folder.
- **Why rejected:** The read is where the value is. Keeping the assistant in the loop, at human pace, keeps both the value and the line.

### Alternative 3: Defer the read until a publisher API exists

- **Description:** Wait for ACBJ, McClatchy, or Axios to offer a licensed feed or API for subscribers.
- **Pros:** Clean.
- **Cons:** None is offered, none is announced, and the owner wants to start now.
- **Why rejected:** Not available.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The line between assistant and bot is drawn once, in behavior the skill can state and test, rather than left to each session's judgment.
- The courier shrinks to something stdlib-simple: a mailbox, three JSON feeds, an RSS parser, and a ledger. No browser, no secrets beyond the mailbox token, no fetch failures to reason about.
- The public-record feeds are also ADR-034's independent sources. The pointer design lands the quorum inputs for free.
- Filing from the read is the owner's accept, so the day's cabinet work happens in one sitting: read, decide, file, attest.

### Negative

- Daily value depends on the owner showing up for the read. A missed morning is a day of pointers with no notes.
- The assistant needs a browser tool inside the owner's session (Claude in Chrome today; a Hermes browser tool later) plus the ffs MCP tools. The read is not available to an agent host without both.
- The Observer and Axios join CBJ as pointer sources under the same rule, so three of the region's editorial outlets contribute headlines only.

### Risks

- **The line is behavioral.** "Reading with me" and "automation" differ in who decides and how fast, not in which API is called. **Mitigation:** the rules in section (2) are the guard; the skill states them as refusals, not preferences; the session refuses bulk requests and logs each article open as a session event so drift is visible in the audit trail.
- **Session tooling changes.** Browser tools come and go across hosts. **Mitigation:** the skill names capabilities (open one URL in the owner's session, read the page text) rather than a vendor tool, and degrades to "the owner opens it and pastes what matters" when no browser tool is present.
