# ADR-035: The morning read is a session with the owner, not a scrape

## Status

Accepted (owner's decision, 2026-09-14)

The owner's words, verbatim: "let's be very careful, I don't want this to act as a bot performing a scrape. I want it to be my personal assistant, conducting our morning read."

## Amendment (2026-09-15)

The first draft of this ADR had the courier's adapter loader refuse bizjournals.com, charlotteobserver.com, and axios.com by domain and removed every fetch option for those publishers. The owner corrected that, verbatim: "whoa... its adapter can't refuse bizjournals, The Observer, and Axios... their terms of service are trying to restrict people who are using their services in nefarious ways. businesses have long clipped articles from the journal for their internal libraries as fair use under license."

What changes: publisher handling is the owner's policy, expressed as configuration, never a refusal baked into code. The software does not adjudicate the owner's license or fair-use position; it records the publishers' terms (spike task_43) so the owner can decide, and it complies with the owner's setting. What stays is the behavioral rule from 2026-09-14: no bot performing a scrape, a personal assistant conducting the morning read. Clipping an article the owner read into their private library is ordinary personal use and is allowed on the owner's cue.

Concretely, this amendment (a) replaces the domain refusal with a per-publisher policy file, `$FFS_DATA_DIR/config/sources.toml`, owner-edited, git-versioned, and hot-reloaded like predicate specs, with two knobs per publisher: `intake = "pointer" | "clip"` (what the courier files from the mailbox: title, url, and date only, or also the body when the mailbox item carries one) and `fetch = "off" | "session" | "scheduled"` (off: never fetch; session: fetch only during an owner-present morning read on the owner's cue; scheduled: the courier may fetch the articles named in the owner's own digest at a human pace and never crawl beyond them). Starter defaults for CBJ, the Observer, and Axios are `intake = "pointer"`, `fetch = "session"`; the owner may set `clip` and `scheduled` and the software complies. (b) Adds "clip this" to the morning read: on the owner's cue the assistant stores the article as a `source.article` with its body under a new classification tier `clip`, which is never federated unless a capability names it explicitly (a local library tier), with `kind: morning_read` provenance carrying the url and the owner-present flag, and an ADR-034 attestation. (c) Restates the remaining rules as behavioral rather than domain-based: no bulk opens, no prefetch outside the read unless `fetch = "scheduled"` is set for that publisher, human pace, only articles named in the owner's own digest, never crawling a site, every open logged. (d) Rewrites "explicitly not built" as "not built by default, enabled by owner configuration". The sections below carry the superseded wording marked as such so the reasoning stays visible.

## Date

2026-09-14

## Context

The newspaper goal (tasks 38 to 47) started from a picture of an agent that receives the Charlotte Business Journal digest, opens every link with the owner's logged-in session, downloads each article, and files what it finds. Spike task_43 tested that picture against reality and found four things, recorded in `docs/research/spikes/task-43-intake-reality.md`:

1. The CBJ digest email carries headlines and links only. No blurbs, no bylines, no names. Zero of fourteen items yield a person or an organization from the email alone. The one engineering gift is that the tracking links embed the real article URL as base64, so canonical URLs decode from the email with no click.
2. bizjournals.com and its RSS host answer 403 to any fetch that is not a browser. The owner's signed-in Chrome session renders full articles (ten paragraphs, about 2,900 characters for the sample), so authenticated browser access works technically.
3. ACBJ's User Agreement (revised 2024-08-13) prohibits using "any bots, cheats, macros, scripts ... or any other automated process" to access the service, and prohibits copying, extracting, or storing content for any purpose "related to data mining and/or the training or operation of any software or service to the extent that it incorporates a large language model." The stated remedy is termination of the account. The Charlotte Observer (McClatchy) and Axios Charlotte carry equivalent clauses.
4. Sources that permit personal automation or are public record exist and are rich: the CLTtoday newsletter (6AM City, personal-use license, full blurbs with names and dates; spike task_42 measured Claude Sonnet 5 at 1.00 precision on people and orgs over five of its emails), Mecklenburg County's permit FeatureServer (REST JSON, no key), Charlotte's Legistar Web API (JSON, no token), SEC EDGAR with a declared User-Agent, and newsroom RSS from Duke Energy and Truist. Two of those feeds already corroborated CBJ headlines from the same week.

ADR-026 drew the line that resolves this. The privacy question there was not "does data leave the machine" but "ambient versus invoked": a daemon that silently ships every note to an API is ambient, a user-configured engine choice is invoked. The same line applies to reading. A scheduled process that opens a subscriber's articles and stores their text is a scrape, whatever session it uses. A person reading the paper with an assistant beside them, opening one article at a time and deciding what to write down, is the owner's own use of the owner's own subscription. The publisher's terms name the first and not the second, and the owner has said plainly which one they want.

## Decision

### (1) Two separate things: the courier and the morning read

**The courier** is deterministic, scheduled, and touches only what may be automated:

- The owner's own mailbox. Each publisher's `intake` setting decides what is filed. Under the starter defaults the CBJ digest is decoded into `source.article` pointers (title, url, date, publication, no content) and CLTtoday items are clipped with their blurb text and the parenthetical attribution each item carries, so ADR-034's quorum can discount a CLTtoday item that is itself a copy of a CBJ report.
- Public-record and permitted feeds: the Mecklenburg County permits FeatureServer, the Charlotte Legistar Web API, SEC EDGAR with a declared User-Agent and the published rate ceiling, newsroom RSS from publishers who intend their releases to be redistributed.

The courier follows the owner's per-publisher policy in `$FFS_DATA_DIR/config/sources.toml` (amendment 2026-09-15): `intake` decides whether a mailbox item is filed as a pointer or clipped with its body, and `fetch` decides whether the courier may fetch the articles named in the owner's own digest on a schedule (`scheduled`), leave them for the morning read (`session`), or never fetch (`off`). The starter file sets CBJ, the Observer, and Axios to `pointer` / `session`; the owner may change either knob. A `scheduled` fetch requests only URLs decoded from the owner's own digest, one at a time at a human pace, under a daily cap, and never crawls beyond them. The courier has no LLM client. task_40 builds it.

Superseded 2026-09-15: "The courier never fetches from bizjournals.com, the Observer, or Axios. It has no browser, no cookie jar, no LLM client." The domain rule is gone; the no-LLM-client rule stays.

**The morning read** is an interactive session. The owner is present, invokes it, and reads at human pace. The assistant walks the day's pointers in order, opens each article in the owner's own signed-in browser one at a time and only when the owner says so, discusses it, and files what the owner wants remembered as the owner's own note: the people, organizations, affiliations, and events, and the owner's take. Never the article text. task_48 writes the skill.

### (2) Rules the assistant MUST follow during the read

- One article at a time, on the owner's cue. No bulk opens: "open all of today's links" is refused as a bulk behavior, whatever the domain. No prefetch outside the read unless the owner set `fetch = "scheduled"` for that publisher in `sources.toml`. Human pace. Only articles named in the owner's own digest; never crawling a site.
- Article bodies are not stored unless the owner says "clip this". On that cue the assistant stores the article as a `source.article` with its body under the `clip` classification tier (local library only; never federated unless a capability names the tier), with `kind: morning_read` provenance carrying the url and the owner-present flag, and an ADR-034 attestation. This is the internal-library practice the owner described. Superseded 2026-09-15: "No caching or storing of article bodies, in the substrate, in scratch files, or in the session log."
- Summaries are spoken in the session. Only the notes the owner chooses are filed, plus any article the owner clipped.
- Provenance on every filed atom records the article url as the source and the session as the origin: `kind: morning_read`, the assistant's identity as the actor, and a flag that the owner was present.
- Because the owner is present and decides, filing from the read is the owner's accept. This is AARM Context-Dependent Allow with the human in the loop live, so proposals from the read may be accepted in-session without a separate quarantine pass. Each such accept auto-emits an ADR-034 attestation with basis `owner_knowledge` (the owner stated it) or `re_read_same_source` (the owner read it in the article).
- Anything the assistant infers rather than the owner states is marked as inference in the filed content, per ADR-027.
- The assistant refuses bulk requests ("open all of today's links", "summarize everything") because they are bulk behavior, not because of the publisher, and says why. Each article open is logged as a session event.
- A publisher with `fetch = "session"` is fetched only during the read, only on cue. A publisher with `fetch = "off"` is never fetched; the owner opens it themselves and pastes what matters.

### (3) The read doubles as the review

The day's pending quarantine items (courier-filed pointers, CLTtoday extractions, permit and Council items, ambiguous resolutions from task_45's resolver, role changes routed by ADR-029) are walked in the same session. ADR-032's inbox file becomes the read's agenda. Spike task_44 therefore measures the read, not a solo review pass: the question is whether the owner would do this daily with an assistant, not whether they would tick forty boxes alone.

### (4) Not built by default, enabled by owner configuration

Amended 2026-09-15. These exist as settings and are off for terms-restricted publishers in the starter `sources.toml`:

- Scheduled fetch of the articles named in the owner's own digest (`fetch = "scheduled"`), at a human pace, under a daily cap, using session cookies the owner exported explicitly or a browser-hosted fetch.
- Clip intake from the mailbox (`intake = "clip"`) and "clip this" during the read.
- LLM extraction over clipped bodies, which runs only over what the owner chose to clip.

What is truly not built, under any setting:

- Crawling a site beyond the articles named in the owner's own digest.
- Any bypass of a login, a paywall, or a bot wall.
- A hard-coded domain denylist or allowlist in code (Alternative 4).

Superseded 2026-09-15: "An unattended CBJ fetcher of any shape. A cookie-jar or headless-browser fetch path. LLM extraction over stored bizjournals.com, Observer, or Axios bodies. Any `fetch.mode` or per-source fetch flag for terms-restricted publishers. The option is removed from task_40, not left off by default."

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Automated full fetch with the owner accepting the account risk

- **Description:** Build the authenticated fetch (cookie jar or the browser extension on a schedule), extract with the LLM engine, and treat the User Agreement as the owner's risk to carry.
- **Pros:** The original picture works end to end with no one present; the cabinet fills itself.
- **Cons:** It is the thing the publisher's terms name. The practical exposure is termination of a paid subscription the owner values. It also makes FFS's first real pipeline an example of the behavior the agent memory convention exists to prevent: an agent acting outside what the human sanctioned.
- **Why rejected:** Rejected by the owner, in the words quoted above.

### Alternative 2: Pointer-only with no reading assistance

- **Description:** The courier files CBJ titles and URLs and the briefing lists them; the owner reads alone and writes notes by hand if they want to.
- **Pros:** Nothing touches the publisher; simplest possible courier.
- **Cons:** Loses the goal. "Read the newspaper end to end and keep the filing cabinets current" was the point; a list of links the owner may or may not open is a bookmark folder.
- **Why rejected:** The read is where the value is. Keeping the assistant in the loop, at human pace, keeps both the value and the line.

### Alternative 3: Defer the read until a publisher API exists

- **Description:** Wait for ACBJ, McClatchy, or Axios to offer a licensed feed or API for subscribers.
- **Pros:** Clean.
- **Cons:** None is offered, none is announced, and the owner wants to start now.
- **Why rejected:** Not available.

### Alternative 4: Hard-coded domain denylist (rejected 2026-09-15)

- **Description:** The courier's adapter loader refuses bizjournals.com, charlotteobserver.com, and axios.com, and the skills carry the same list, so no configuration can make the software fetch or clip from those publishers.
- **Pros:** The line cannot be crossed by accident; nothing to configure.
- **Cons:** The software decides for the owner what the owner's subscription permits. It treats a terms clause aimed at nefarious use as binding on ordinary personal use, and it forbids the long-standing practice of clipping articles into an internal library under a subscription. It also has to be edited in code every time the owner adds a publisher.
- **Why rejected:** The owner's words: "its adapter can't refuse bizjournals, The Observer, and Axios... their terms of service are trying to restrict people who are using their services in nefarious ways. businesses have long clipped articles from the journal for their internal libraries as fair use under license." The judgment is the owner's; the software records the terms and complies with the owner's setting.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The line between assistant and bot is drawn once, in behavior the skill can state and test, rather than left to each session's judgment.
- The courier shrinks to something stdlib-simple: a mailbox, three JSON feeds, an RSS parser, and a ledger. No browser, no secrets beyond the mailbox token, no fetch failures to reason about.
- The public-record feeds are also ADR-034's independent sources. The pointer design lands the quorum inputs for free.
- Filing from the read is the owner's accept, so the day's cabinet work happens in one sitting: read, decide, file, attest.

### Negative

- Daily value depends on the owner showing up for the read. A missed morning is a day of pointers with no notes.
- The assistant needs a browser tool inside the owner's session (Claude in Chrome today; a Hermes browser tool later) plus the ffs MCP tools. The read is not available to an agent host without both.
- Under the starter defaults the Observer and Axios join CBJ as pointer sources, so three of the region's editorial outlets contribute headlines only until the owner changes `sources.toml`. Clipping during the read fills the library one article at a time, at the owner's pace.

### Risks

- **The line is behavioral.** "Reading with me" and "automation" differ in who decides and how fast, not in which API is called or which domain is fetched. **Mitigation:** the rules in section (2) are the guard; the skill states them as refusals of bulk behavior, not of publishers; the session refuses bulk requests and logs each article open as a session event so drift is visible in the audit trail. A `scheduled` fetch is bounded the same way: only the owner's digest, one at a time, a daily cap, every request logged.
- **The owner's settings carry the owner's risk.** `sources.toml` lets the owner enable scheduled fetch and clip intake for publishers whose terms restrict automation. **Mitigation:** the starter file ships those publishers at `pointer` / `session`, the terms are quoted in the spike note the owner can read, and the config file's comments say once what each knob does. It does not moralize and it does not refuse.
- **Session tooling changes.** Browser tools come and go across hosts. **Mitigation:** the skill names capabilities (open one URL in the owner's session, read the page text) rather than a vendor tool, and degrades to "the owner opens it and pastes what matters" when no browser tool is present.
